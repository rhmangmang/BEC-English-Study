## Drill 1: Negative Statement
- **Prompt:** 我对那个新提议的顾虑并不是预算，而是我们的团队目前精力太分散了。 (the new proposal, spread too thin)
- **Student Translation:** My concern with the new proposal isn't about the budget, is about that our team is spread too thin now
- **Feedback:** 
  - Idiomatic & Relevance: `My concern with the new proposal` is perfectly used. `spread too thin` is perfectly reused.
  - Grammar & Logic: "isn't about... is about that" is a bit awkward. "isn't the budget, but that our team is..." is much better. There's a missing subject in the second clause "is about that" -> "it is that". 'Now' is okay, 'currently' is slightly more professional.

## Drill 2: Special Question
- **Prompt:** 从架构的角度来看，你对我们目前的支付系统有什么顾虑吗？
- **Student Translation:** From an architecture point of view, what's your concern with our current payment system?
- **Feedback:**
  - Idiomatic & Relevance: Excellent reuse of `From an ... point of view`. Perfect application of `concern with` to ask for a technical critique.
  - Grammar & Logic: Flawless. `architecture point of view` is natural and correct. Plural `what are your concerns with` might sound slightly more common for open-ended questions, but singular `what is your concern` is totally fine.

## Drill 3: Tag Question

- **Feedback:**
  - Idiomatic & Relevance: `my concern with this design` is excellent.

## LP 2 Drill 2: Special Question
- **Prompt:** 你懂支持那个做法的理由是什么吗？
- **Student Translation:** 
  - Attempt 1: What do you see the argument for it?
  - Attempt 2: What the argument for it do you see?
- **Feedback:**
  - Idiomatic & Relevance: Very close. The user understands the concept of binding "what" with the noun. 
  - Grammar & Logic: "What" as an interrogative adjective means "which/what kind of". It directly replaces the article "the". Thus, "What the argument" is grammatically incorrect (double determiners). It should simply be "What argument". The full sentence becomes "What argument do you see for it?" or "What argument for it do you see?". I'll point out the article issue using an analogy (What color vs What the color).

## LP 2 Drill 3: Tag Question

- **Student Translation:** To be honest, you didn't actually see the argument for it, did you?
- **Feedback:**
  - Idiomatic & Relevance: Perfect use of `To be honest` and `actually` for emphasis. 
  - Grammar & Logic: Flawless polarity match: negative main clause (`didn't`) -> positive tag (`did you`). Using past tense `didn't` is correct here as it refers to the moment the argument was presented.

## LP 2 Drill 5: Virtual Mood / Subjunctive

- **Feedback:**

  - Grammar & Logic: 🏆 **Formal Subject Mastery**: Successfully used the "It would be confusing why..." structure to shift the focus from the speaker to the logic itself.
